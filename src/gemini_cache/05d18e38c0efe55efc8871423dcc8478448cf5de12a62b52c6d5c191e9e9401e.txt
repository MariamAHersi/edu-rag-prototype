  "task_description": "ASSIGNMENT STRUCTURE: This assignment comprises five interconnected tasks. Tasks 1–4 are preparatory work and, Task 5 is the final assessed presentation that integrates all prior work. Assessed component (60% slides + 40% Presentation). TASK 1: SELECTION OF E-COMMERCE PLATFORM. You will select a distinctive e-commerce application or website for detailed research on user experience. This will form the focus of all subsequent tasks. Step 1: Choose Your Platform: Select an e-commerce platform that interests you. Do not select Amazon. The following link provides a starting list of options, but you are not limited to these: https://www.tigren.com/blog/ecommerce-companies/ Step 2: Document the Platform: Gather basic information about your chosen platform: Name and description: What is it? Who uses it? Key features: What are 3–5 discrete, user-facing features? User roles: Who are the primary user types (e.g. buyer, seller, admin)? Business model: How does it make money? Reach: Geographic scope, user base size (if available). TASK 2: USER EXPERIENCE RESEARCH – EXPLORE METHOD. You will choose one Explore' UX research method and use it to document and analyse a specific feature of your chosen e-commerce platform. Step 1: Identify and List Features: A feature is \"a discrete piece of functionality desired by stakeholders.\" Identify 3–5 features of your chosen platform and, for each, identify which user role(s) benefit from it. Example: Feature: Product reviews; User roles: Buyer (to inform purchase decisions), Seller (to receive feedback). Step 2: Choose an 'Explore' Method and Conduct Research: Select one of the following UX research methods and apply it to analyse a single feature in depth: Persona: Create 1–2 detailed personas representing your target users. Journey Mapping: Map the end-to-end user journey for a specific task involving your chosen feature. User Story Mapping: Break down user goals into user stories and tasks. Step 3: Conduct User Interviews: Interview at least 2 users of your chosen e-commerce platform to gather qualitative data. These users should represent different user roles or perspectives (e.g. a frequent buyer and a first-time user). Topics to explore: How do they currently use the feature you are analysing? What problems or friction points do they encounter? What would improve their experience? Documentation: Record key insights, direct quotes, and observations. Step 4: Analyse and Synthesise: Use your chosen method to synthesise your interview data. You may examine the same feature from multiple user perspectives (e.g. both buyer and seller viewpoints). Your chosen UX method; 1–2 key personas, user stories, or journey map; Summary of user needs and pain points (informed by interviews). TASK 3: USABILITY TESTING. You will conduct a ‘think-aloud' usability test’ to evaluate a feature of your chosen e-commerce platform and gather evidence for recommendations. Step 1: Plan Your Test: Feature to test: Use the feature you identified in Task 2; Participants: Recruit 2–3 participants (can include the same users as Task 2, or new ones); Tasks to perform: Design 2–3 realistic tasks that use your chosen feature. Step 2: Execute the Test: Use the Think-Aloud Test Template available on Moodle; Ask participants to verbalise their thoughts as they perform tasks; Observe and note difficulties, confusion, successes, and unexpected behaviour; Ask clarifying questions. Step 3: Analyse the Data and Generate Recommendations: Identify usability issues from your observations; Categorise issues by severity (critical, major, minor); For each issue, propose concrete recommendations for improvement; Categorise recommendations as: 'Easy wins' Quick, low-cost fixes with notable benefits, 'Sound investments': More complex improvements that deliver substantial long-term value. TASK 4: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY, LEGAL/ETHICAL, AND ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS. You will conduct critical research into the societal and environmental impacts of your chosen e-commerce platform. 1. Key Facts: Expand on details about your case study: Number of users / user base demographics, Geographic reach, Annual revenue or funding (if public), Company ownership and structure. 2. Intellectual Property: Examine intellectual property (IP) crucial to your case study: Software licenses, Content licenses, Trademarks and patents, Critical analysis. 3. Legal, Ethical, and Social Issues: Identify and analyse trade-offs in the platform's design and operation: Privacy, Labour rights, Human rights, Surveillance and bias, Emerging technology issues, Trade-offs. 4. Environmental Impacts: Consider the environmental footprint of the platform and its effects: Computational impacts, Service design impacts, Net environmental effect. 5. Conclusion and Synthesis: Summarise your research. What are your key findings? Can you apply your findings to draw conclusions and/or make recommendations for your case study and perhaps more widely? TASK 5: FINAL PRESENTATION. You will produce a single, cohesive PowerPoint presentation that integrates Tasks 1–4 and deliver it live in class. Presentation Structure (Suggested): 1. Recommended slide breakdown (total ~10–12 content slides); 2. Title Slide (Student name, student ID, module code, submission date); 3. Platform Overview (1 slide); 4. UX Research Method & Findings (2–3 slides); 5. Usability Testing (2–3 slides); 6. Intellectual Property Analysis (1 slide); 7. Legal, Ethical, and Social Analysis (2 slides); 8. Environmental Impacts (1 slide); 9. Conclusions and Recommendations (1 slide); 10. References Slide (1 slide)."
