A short reflection on your experience of the module and any lessons learned that you will apply to your Final Year Project.\n\nTask 1: STAR Story\nThe STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is a commonly used way to evaluate candidates during job interviews. It is comprised of 4 steps:\n• Situation: Describe the situation and when it took place.\n• Task: Explain the task and what was the goal.\n• Action: Provide details about the action you took to attain this.\n• Result: Conclude with the result of your action.\nThe STAR method is preferred by many job recruiters because it gathers information about candidates' real experiences, and what they actually did in real situations.\nA STAR story is not a theoretical explanation of what you think should be done, or where you tell the interviewer what you think is the desired ‘right answer’.\nWe are asking you to do this to give you an opportunity to practice this method and prepare you for future interviews. We also want to help you understand how you can use your experiences at university to illustrate your personal strengths and allow you to stand out from other candidates by telling stories about real situations.\nYou can find many useful examples online, for example:\nhttps://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method\nhttps://www.betterup.com/blog/star-interview-method\n\nYour Task:\nPlease choose one of the following questions and answer it using the STAR method. That means you should explicitly write a paragraph about each of the sections (i.e., describe the situation, describe the task, describe the action you took to address this task, and describe the result).\nThings to remember:\n• You must write about a real situation that you faced during your coursework.\n• Do not use AI for this assignment. If you hand in an answer that is generic and which your lecturer cannot recognize from the work you have done, your work will not be able to pass.\n• You should not name names in your group or apportion blame. If you need to refer to something somebody else has done, please just refer to ‘a group member’.\n• You should be able to tell your STAR story from a positive perspective. Even if things went wrong, a good STAR story can show that you took effective action to resolve a difficult situation.\nThe questions: Please choose one:\n• Tell me about a time when there was a disagreement in your team.\n• Tell me about a time when you were stuck on a task.\n• Tell me about a time when your project seemed to be stuck in a rut and not moving ahead at the required pace.\n• Tell me about a time you were concerned that a deadline might be missed.\n• Tell me about a time when you had to correct a colleague's work.\n• Tell me about a time when you had to challenge the actions of someone in your team.\n• Tell me about a time when you made a wrong decision\n• Tell me about a time when you had to shift priorities\nRemember STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Your answer must have a well-defined short paragraph about each of these.\n\nTask 2: Module Reflection\nPlease write a paragraph (do not use AI) in which you consider the main lessons learned from this module.
