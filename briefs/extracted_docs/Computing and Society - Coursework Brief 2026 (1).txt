| Title: E-Commerce UX, Usability, and Societal Impacts: Assessment description ASSIGNMENT STRUCTURE This assignment comprises five interconnected tasks. Tasks 1–4 are preparatory work and, Task 5 is the final assessed presentation that integrates all prior work. Assessed component (60% slides + 40% Presentation) TASK 1: SELECTION OF E-COMMERCE PLATFORM You will select a distinctive e-commerce application or website for detailed research on user experience. This will form the focus of all subsequent tasks. Step 1: Choose Your Platform Select an e-commerce platform that interests you. Do not select Amazon. The following link provides a starting list of options, but you are not limited to these: https://www.tigren.com/blog/ecommerce-companies/ Step 2: Document the Platform Gather basic information about your chosen platform: • Name and description: What is it? Who uses it? | - |

          TASK 2: USER  EXPERIENCE   RESEARCH    – EXPLORE  METHOD                
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
          You will choose one Explore' UX research method and use it to document and analyse a specific
          feature of your chosen e-commerce platform.                             
                                                                                  
          Step 1: Identify and List Features                                      
                                                                                  
          A feature is "a discrete piece of functionality desired by stakeholders." Identify 3–5 features of
          your chosen platform and, for each, identify which user role(s) benefit from it.
                                                                                  
          Example:                                                                
            •  Feature: Product reviews                                           
                                                                                  
            •  User roles: Buyer (to inform purchase decisions), Seller (to receive feedback)
                                                                                  
          Step 2: Choose an 'Explore' Method and Conduct Research                 
          Select one of the following UX research methods and apply it to analyse a single feature in
                                                                                  
          depth:                                                                  
          Persona                                                                 
                                                                                  
            •  Create 1–2 detailed personas representing your target users.       
               Use the Nielsen Norman Group resource:                             
                                                                                  
               https://www.nngroup.com/articles/personas-are-living-documents/    
          Journey Mapping                                                         
                                                                                  
            •  Map the end-to-end user journey for a specific task involving your chosen feature.
               Use: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/         
                                                                                  
          User Story Mapping                                                      
                                                                                  
            •  Break down user goals into user stories and tasks.                 
               Use: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-story-mapping/          
                                                                                  
          Use class resources (Moodle, Week 2) and in-class activities to support your method choice.
                                                                                  
          Step 3: Conduct User Interviews                                         
                                                                                  
          Interview at least 2 users of your chosen e-commerce platform to gather qualitative data. These
          users should represent different user roles or perspectives (e.g. a frequent buyer and a first-
          time user).                                                             
                                                                                  
          Topics to explore:                                                      
                                                                                  
            •  How do they currently use the feature you are analysing?           
            •  What problems or friction points do they encounter?

          Documentation: Record key insights, direct quotes, and observations.    
          Step 4: Analyse and Synthesise                                          
                                                                                  
          Use your chosen method to synthesise your interview data. You may examine the same feature
          from multiple user perspectives (e.g. both buyer and seller viewpoints).

          TASK 3: USABILITY  TESTING                                              
                                                                                  
          You will conduct a ‘think-aloud' usability test’ to evaluate a feature of your chosen e-
          commerce platform and gather evidence for recommendations.              
                                                                                  
          Step 1: Plan Your Test                                                  
                                                                                  
            •  Feature to test: Use the feature you identified in Task 2          
            •  Participants: Recruit 2–3 participants (can include the same users as Task 2, or new
               ones)                                                              
                                                                                  
            •  Tasks to perform: Design 2–3 realistic tasks that use your chosen feature
          Step 2: Execute the Test                                                
                                                                                  
            •  Use the Think-Aloud Test Template available on Moodle              
            •  Ask participants to verbalise their thoughts as they perform tasks 
                                                                                  
            •  Observe and note difficulties, confusion, successes, and unexpected behaviour
            •  Ask clarifying questions (e.g. "Why did you click there?" or "What are you looking for?")
                                                                                  
          Step 3: Analyse the Data and Generate Recommendations                   
                                                                                  
            •  Identify usability issues from your observations                   
            •  Categorise issues by severity (critical, major, minor)             
            •  For each issue, propose concrete recommendations for improvement   
            •  Categorise recommendations as:                                     
                                                                                  
                 -  'Easy wins' Quick, low-cost fixes with notable benefits       
                 -  'Sound investments': More complex improvements that deliver substantial long-
                    term value

          You will conduct critical research into the societal and environmental impacts of your chosen e-
          commerce platform.

          Expand on details about your case study:                                
            •  Number of users / user base demographics                           
            •  Geographic reach                                                   
            •  Annual revenue or funding (if public)                              
                                                                                  
            •  Company ownership and structure                                    
          2. Intellectual Property                                                
                                                                                  
          Examine intellectual property (IP) crucial to your case study:          
            •  Software licenses: Is the platform open-source, proprietary, or a hybrid?
            •  Content licenses: What rights do users have to their uploaded content (photos, reviews,
                                                                                  
               descriptions)?                                                     
            •  Trademarks and patents: What IP protections does the platform hold?
            •  Critical analysis: How do IP regimes affect users, usage patterns, and the business
               model? Do they create competitive advantages or barriers to entry? 
          3. Legal, Ethical, and Social Issues                                    
                                                                                  
          Identify and analyse trade-offs in the platform's design and operation: 
            •  Privacy: What data is collected? How is it used? Are there clear consent mechanisms?
                                                                                  
            •  Labour rights: How are workers treated (e.g. warehouse staff, content moderators,
               delivery drivers)?                                                 
            •  Human rights: Are there supply-chain or third-party concerns?      
            •  Surveillance and bias: Does the platform use algorithmic decision-making? Are there
                                                                                  
               known biases?                                                      
            •  Emerging technology issues: If applicable, discuss AI, recommendation algorithms, or
               automated moderation                                               
            •  Trade-offs: Have rights (labour, privacy, autonomy) been compromised for business
                                                                                  
               goals? Conversely, has the platform adopted inclusive or fair-trade models, even if they
               increase costs?                                                    
                                                                                  
          4. Environmental Impacts                                                
          Consider the environmental footprint of the platform and its effects:   
                                                                                  
            •  Computational impacts: Energy use of servers, data centres, infrastructure
            •  Service design impacts: How has platform design influenced user behaviour and
                                                                                  
               environmental outcomes?                                            
                 -  Example: Uber and Zipcar reduce private car ownership; fashion e-commerce
                    platforms may increase consumption and waste                  
                 -  Example: Delivery services generate packaging waste and carbon emissions
            •  Net environmental effect: Is the platform's overall environmental impact positive,
                                                                                  
               negative, or mixed?                                                
                                                                                  
          5. Conclusion and Synthesis                                             
          Summarise your research. What are your key findings? Can you apply your findings to draw
                                                                                  
          conclusions and/or make recommendations for your case study and perhaps more widely?

          Evidence and Referencing                                                
            •  All claims must be supported by credible sources:                  
                                                                                  
                 -  Academic journals (via Springer Link, Google Scholar, etc.)   
                 -  Industry reports and case studies                             
                 -  News articles and investigative journalism                    
                 -  Company transparency reports or official statements           
                 -  Government or regulatory documents                            
                                                                                  
          Use IEEE referencing format (numbered citations in brackets [1], [2], etc., with a numbered
          reference list at the end).                                             
                                                                                  
          TASK 5: FINAL PRESENTATION                                              
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
          You will produce a single, cohesive PowerPoint presentation that integrates Tasks 1–4 and
          deliver it live in class.                                               
                                                                                  
          Presentation Structure (Suggested)                                      
            1. Recommended slide breakdown (total ~10–12 content slides)          
                                                                                  
            2. Title Slide (Student name, student ID, module code, submission date)
            3. Platform Overview (1 slide) – Name, description, key features      
            4. UX Research Method & Findings (2–3 slides) – Method chosen, user personas/stories,
               interview insights, pain points                                    
            5. Usability Testing (2–3 slides) – Methodology, key issues, recommendations
                                                                                  
            6. Intellectual Property Analysis (1 slide) – Key IP, licences, protections
            7. Legal, Ethical, and Social Analysis (2 slides) – Privacy, labour, bias, trade-offs
            8. Environmental Impacts** (1 slide) – Computational and design impacts
            9. Conclusions and Recommendations (1 slide) – Summary, key findings, future directions
            10. References Slide (1 slide) – Numbered IEEE reference list         
                                                                                  
          Design and Professional Quality Standards                               
                                                                                  
          -Font size: Minimum 18pt for body text; 28pt+ for headings              
                                                                                  
          - Readability: Use high contrast between text and background; avoid walls of text
                                                                                  
          - Visuals: Include screenshots of the platform, personas, journey maps, process diagrams, and
          data visualisations (if applicable)                                     
                                                                                  
          - Consistency: Use a coherent colour scheme and font family throughout  
          - Citations: Each claim should be backed by an inline citation (e.g. "[1]" placed after the claim)
