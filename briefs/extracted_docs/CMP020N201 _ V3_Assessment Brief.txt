| Assessment Requirements  <br> Overview <br> You will build a small application using Java. <br> The coursework should be delivered in 3 sprints. In each sprint, you will add new functionality and improve your code using the knowledge you will gain as the module progresses. <br>  <br> Planning and documentation are as important as working code. We are looking for a professional approach that considers readability, maintainability, conformance to standards, and correct operation. You will supply detailed documentation as well as code. <br> Please note that this is an individual piece of work. You will be asked to present your work in person to a lecturer and answer questions about your solution. You will have one practice assessment of sprint 1 (this won’t be graded – it’s just to help you check your progress) and two graded assessments. Sprint 2 will be marked in code reviews in class. You can only be marked for your sprint 2 if you have attended in-class assessment of sprint 1. <br> Task <br> Please choose one of the following projects to develop in your coursework. You should choose one project and stick to it throughout the module, fulfilling each of the sprint requirements in the context of your chosen project. Think carefully about which topic interests you most, as you will work on it throughout the term. <br>  <br> Your overall aim is to produce well-architected OOP code that shows an understanding of the problem domain, created using standard OOP techniques, and properly documented both within the code and in accompanying design documents. For each sprint, there will be well-defined basic requirements that must be met to achieve a pass, and advanced features that you can implement if you are aiming for higher marks. <br>  <br> We expect your code to conform to established coding standards as detailed here: https://google.github.io/styleguide/javaguide.html <br> Projects <br> SpareB&B <br> Users can book different kinds of accommodation. You can make any kind of accommodation available, for example, flats, hotels, luxury villas, or sofas. Your accommodations may have different facilities, such as kitchens or washing machines. <br> ZipAbout <br> Users can rent shared lightweight electric transport vehicles – bikes, scooters, skateboards, segway’s and other vehicles, perhaps yet to be invented. For some vehicles, users will need equipment such as helmets, kneepads, or gloves to use them. <br> LibraryOfStuff <br> Users can borrow all kinds of items - tools, sewing machines, phone chargers, bicycle pumps – little things we often need but can’t lay our hands on. Items can be categorized by the part of the home in which they are usually used: kitchen, garden, workshop, or ‘out and about’. <br>  <br> Sprint 1 – Basic Classes - <br> FORMATIVE assessment in lab sessions: <br> Submission deadline: 24-October-2025 <br>  <br> Consider the objects that can be booked, rented, or borrowed in your application. Identify their methods and properties. <br> Use the OOP principles of Abstraction, Encapsulation, Inheritance, and Polymorphism to design your classes <br> Create a class diagram showing the relationships between your classes. <br> Write well-documented Java code to implement your classes. Minimum requirements should be: <br> a constructor <br> some properties <br> a print () method which gives some user-friendly output. <br> Provide a Main class and main method that you can run in the console, in which: <br> At least 3 objects of different types are created in your code <br> A user-friendly listing of the objects can be printed to the console <br> For those aiming for higher marks, also consider the additional information, e.g., facilities, equipment, and hobbies. Consider how composition can be used to model these in your system. Add them to your class diagram and code.  <br>  <br> If you are seeking comprehensive feedback on your project activities, then please submit sprint 1 no later than 27-October-2025. Please hand in your work in Moodle and seek feedback during the week 5 and 6 lab sessions. <br>  <br> Sprint 2:  <br> Booking functionality  <br>   Submission Deadline: 21-November-2025 <br> 50% of marks <br>  <br> Implement a booking system for your chosen project in Java. You do not need to implement booking for specific dates or durations, but it should be possible for a user to book and release items. Once an item is booked, it should not be available for booking by any other user. <br> NOTE:  <br> You do not have to capture any user input at this stage of development.  <br> Basic requirements <br> Provide a set of user stories to cover your provided functionality <br> Provide a class diagram showing the relevant classes and relationships. <br> Your design and code should incorporate the sprint 1 requirements above, plus basic booking functionality. <br> Each class containing at least 3 properties/attributes and 3 methods. <br> Provide a Main class/method in which at minimum: <br> At least 3 objects of different types are created directly in your code. <br> At least 3 users are created directly in your code. <br> In code, show that a booking can be made of an item by a user. <br> Show in your code that if another user tries to book the same item, they are politely informed that the item is unavailable. <br> User-friendly output can be printed showing correct information about items and the booking status at any particular time. <br>  <br> Advanced requirements <br>  <br> Basic requirements plus... <br>  <br> Your design incorporates the additional information, e.g., Facilities, equipment, and hobbies for bookable items <br> Include at least one design pattern, accompanied by a short explanation of how it has been implemented, and/or <br> Devise a feature of your own choice that demonstrates your deeper understanding of design patterns. Perhaps by rewarding your application users in some way with loyalty points or a random prize draw, or perhaps by adding features to your application, such as managing cleaning or maintenance, or providing output in different formats (these are just examples). <br>  <br> Sprint 3 - GUI development  <br>  <br> Submission Deadline: 7-January 2026 <br> 50% of marks <br> Assessment based on demo and viva will be held in week 13. <br> Create and document a GUI for your application using JavaFX.  <br>  <br> Basic requirements <br> Documentation <br>  <br> Provide a schematic ‘wireframe’ that provides the specification for your GUI <br> Provide a set of user stories and class diagrams that document the provided functionality. Provide screenshots of your finished application. <br> Write a conclusion to your documentation in which you identify the programming paradigms that you have used to create your application, e.g., object-oriented, declarative, functional, and describe why each is suitable for different tasks. <br> Code <br>  <br> Implement basic button and list functionalities taught in class with some original design and formatting. <br> Use the classes you have created in sprint 2 to create some basic functionality where you list bookable items. <br>  <br> Advanced requirements <br>  <br> Use the classes you have created in sprint 2, including your design patterns, to implement the listing and selection of items, and provide feedback to the user. | Ethical Requirements <br> This assessment does not require students to handle real-world personal data, confidential information, or sensitive organisational materials. All development work is carried out in a simulated academic environment, and projects are self-contained and for educational purposes only. <br> Confidentiality and Anonymity <br> Any example data (e.g., names of users, booking records, or borrowed items) should be fictitious or anonymised. <br> Code demonstrations and viva assessments will remain confidential between the student and teaching staff. <br> Academic and Professional Standards <br> All submissions must comply with academic integrity requirements (no plagiarism, collusion, or code sharing without attribution). <br> Referencing and acknowledgement of third-party resources (e.g., tutorials, libraries, frameworks) must follow university guidance. <br> Students are expected to follow professional coding standards (e.g., Google Java Style Guide) and conduct themselves in line with good software engineering practice. <br> Legal and Organisational Considerations <br> The assessment must comply with UK copyright law and university regulations on academic work. <br> Students must not include or distribute copyrighted material without permission. <br> No collection, processing, or storage of real personal data is permitted. <br> Ethical Approval <br> As this is a classroom-based, individual programming project with fictitious scenarios, formal ethical approval is not required. <br> If a student wishes to extend their project to include real user data or deploy it beyond the classroom context, they must seek prior ethical approval from the university’s ethics committee and BCS code of Conduct. | - |
| Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) <br> ACADEMIC MISCONDUCT: Your submission for this coursework will be scrutinized for plagiarism, collusion, and other forms of academic misconduct. Please ensure that the work that you submit is your own and that you have cited and referenced appropriately, to avoid having to attend an academic misconduct hearing. You can find more information about academic integrity and how to mitigate plagiarism here: https://roehampton.libwizard.com/f/academic_integrity <br>  <br> The assessment is designed so that the use of AI during the assessment is possible. You must acknowledge any use of AI and appropriately cite all AI generated outputs. Please make sure you read and understand the assessment guidelines and ask your Module Leader if you have any questions. You can find the guidance on the use of AI here.  <br>  <br> Understanding Your Work <br> Do not submit any code, diagrams, or documentation that you cannot explain. During the viva/oral examination, you will be asked to discuss and justify your design choices, implementation, and testing. If you include a single line of code or design element that you do not understand, it will be treated as passing off someone else’s work (including that generated by AI) as your own. This constitutes academic misconduct. Penalties may include receiving a zero for this assessment component and being reported under the University’s Academic Misconduct procedures. Academic misconduct has also been shown to negatively impact students’ employment opportunities. <br> Generative AI <br> You may use Generative AI responsibly and ethically as part of your learning process. AI tools can help you clarify concepts, refine your understanding, and explore alternative solutions but they should augment, not replace, your own thinking. Treat AI as you would a supportive lecturer: <br> Ask it to explain concepts in different ways (e.g., visual, example-driven). <br> Use it to check your understanding by asking for alternative explanations or coding feedback. <br> Do not ask AI to simply generate full solutions for you, this undermines your learning and will be evident in your code review and viva examination. <br> Example AI Prompts for Learning <br> “Act as a Computer Science lecturer. I am developing a booking system in Java. Can you explain how to apply polymorphism in this context?” <br> “Please show me a UML class diagram example for inheritance with accommodation types.” <br> “I’m unsure how to implement a simple booking release function. Can you explain the logic step by step?” <br> “Please review my Java method [paste method] and suggest improvements for readability and efficiency.” <br> Grading and Oral Examination <br> Marks are awarded not only for working programs and complete documentation (e.g., class diagrams, wireframes, user stories) but also for your ability to: <br> Justify your design decisions. <br> Demonstrate efficiency, simplicity, and creativity in your solutions. <br> Apply good coding style and professional standards. <br> Your oral examination (viva) will be used to confirm your understanding of the code you have written and to assess your ability to explain, adapt, and improve it. The viva is part of the marking process, in line with the assessment rubrics provided. | - |
